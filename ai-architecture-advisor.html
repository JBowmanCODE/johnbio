      Why Most AI Architectures Overbuild on Day One
      
        
      Teams routinely jump straight to multi-agent frameworks or custom fine-tuning when a cached system prompt with strict JSON outputs or a deterministic workflow graph would ship in a week and cost a tenth as much to run. Every extra layer you add - vector databases, rerankers, open-ended tool loops, self-hosted GPU clusters - brings its own failure modes and on-call burden.
      
        
      This advisor applies a deterministic principal-architect rubric across 10 engineering constraints to recommend the simplest pattern that satisfies your SLA, privacy boundary and risk tolerance, then hands you a clickable architecture diagram and a commit-ready MADR you can take straight into a design review.
      
      
      

      
      

        
      
          
            Frequently Asked Questions
            
          
          
      
            
      
              
      
                
      Why does this tool often advise against building an autonomous AI agent?
      
                
      Open-ended agent loops add latency, token burn and unpredictable failure modes. If your task follows a known sequence of lookups and checks, a deterministic state graph with scoped model calls is cheaper, faster to debug and safer in production than an unconstrained tool-calling loop.
      
              
      
              
      
                
      When should I pick Hybrid RAG instead of long-context prompting?
      
                
      Keep documents in a cached prompt while your active corpus fits comfortably under 100k tokens and changes slowly. Move to Hybrid RAG (BM25 lexical search plus dense embeddings and a reranker) once your corpus grows past 100k tokens, needs per-user access control or updates daily.
      
              
      
              
      
                
      Will fine-tuning stop an LLM from hallucinating or fix outdated company facts?
      
                
      No. Fine-tuning teaches format, tone, domain style and fast classification at high volume, not live facts. Storing changing policies or pricing inside model weights creates stale answers that you cannot easily patch without retraining. Use retrieval or live tool calls for mutable facts.
      
              
      
              
      
                
      Does my project name or architecture specification leave my browser?
      
                
      No. Every scoring rule, SVG diagram, failure-mode checklist and Markdown ADR runs locally in your browser with vanilla JavaScript. Nothing is uploaded to a server or sent to an external model API.
      
              
      
              
      
                
      Can I commit the generated ADR straight into my Git repository?
      
                
      Yes. The output follows the Markdown Architecture Decision Record (MADR) format with Context, Decision, Alternatives Considered, Guardrails, Consequences and Eval Metrics. Click Download .md or Copy ADR to drop it directly into docs/adr/ in your repository.
      
              
      
              
      
                
      How does the shareable URL work without a backend database?
      
                
      Clicking Copy Shareable URL encodes your 10 constraint settings and project title into standard URL query parameters. Anyone opening that link loads the exact same pattern score, interactive diagram and ADR locally in their own browser.

            Key Points
            
          
          
      
            
        
              
      • Minimum viable complexity wins in production. The scoring engine starts from the lowest-maintenance pattern that meets your hard constraints and only steps up to RAG, workflows, bounded agents or fine-tuning when the numbers justify it.
        • 
              
      • Deterministic rules, zero API calls. There is no black-box LLM guessing your stack; every recommendation is reproducible, transparent and visible in the 6-pattern comparison matrix.
        • 
              
      • Explicit anti-pattern callouts. Knowing what not to build saves months of wasted sprint capacity - especially avoiding vector DBs for small static corpora, fine-tuning for volatile facts and unbounded agents on write actions.
        • 
              
      • Component-level failure analysis. Every node in the inline SVG diagram maps to concrete implementation notes, latency budgets, failure modes and observability metrics.
        • 
              
      • Ready-to-commit MADR output. Export clean Markdown with revisit triggers, governance notes and eval thresholds straight into version control.
