## What do I build at JohnB.io?

More than 20 of them are live and none ask you to sign up: the [T&Cs Simplifier](https://johnb.io/tcs-simplifier), the [iGaming Compliance Map](https://johnb.io/igaming-compliance), the [LLMs.txt Generator](https://johnb.io/llms-generator), an [AI Copyeditor](https://johnb.io/editor), and a free 35-lesson [AI and Machine Learning course](https://johnb.io/course/) with a certificate at the end.

Every one is a static HTML page plus a Cloudflare Worker that holds the API key and the rate limit. No framework, no build step, no package manager. That's a deliberate choice and I'd defend it: a page that loads in under a second and still works when JavaScript fails is worth more than the same tool wrapped in 400kb of React.

That principle got tested on this page. The skills panels above used to be generated by JavaScript, which meant every AI crawler that reads the site without running scripts saw six unlabelled buttons and nothing else. They're rendered in the HTML now.

## Why does 20 years in iGaming matter for AI work?

Because iGaming is one of the few industries where a bad automated decision comes with a regulator attached. Send the wrong bonus message to a self-excluded customer and it isn't a bug, it's a fine.

So the questions I ask about any AI deployment come in a fixed order: what happens when the model gets it wrong, who signs it off, and what the audit trail looks like. That's why I built the [AI Governance Framework](https://johnb.io/rai) and the compliance map before I built anything more interesting.

The failure I see most often is a company buying an LLM licence and calling it a strategy. Most of the value sits in the dull part around the model: who reviews the output, what gets logged, which decisions a human still has to make. Nobody wants to hear that in a kick-off meeting.

## What did 88 million AI music views actually pay?

£600. Between 2024 and early 2026 I made 60 AI-generated tracks, distributed them through SoundON Global, and they picked up 88 million views. Nine of them passed a million each. The total payout was six hundred pounds.

I keep coming back to that number because it's the cleanest lesson in AI economics I've had first hand, and it complicates the point I made earlier about building things yourself. Generating output is now nearly free. Distribution isn't, and reach and revenue stopped being the same measurement somewhere around 2024.
