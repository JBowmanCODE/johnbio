Why Most Enterprise AI Pilots Stall After Demo Day

Every Head of AI has the same inbox problem: twenty department heads asking for chatbots, autonomous agents and "AI-powered" dashboards, backed by vendor ROI slides that assume 100% staff adoption and zero human review time. Half of those requests fall apart the moment Legal asks about GDPR, Data Engineering opens the source tables, or Finance checks the real run cost.

The AI Pilot Killer Matrix replaces fluffy Impact vs Effort sticky notes with five operational axes that decide whether a pilot survives production, plus a plain-code detector that catches jobs a SQL query or Python script should handle instead. Drag the global Year-1 adoption slider down to 60%, add honest QA overhead, and export a ranked Steering Committee Pack your CFO can actually defend.

Frequently Asked Questions
Why not use a standard Impact vs Effort matrix for AI requests?
Generic impact-effort grids hide the four things that actually kill enterprise AI pilots: messy source data, high error blast radius, regulatory sign-off delays and people ignoring the tool after launch. Two requests with identical effort estimates behave completely differently if one writes internal draft notes and the other triggers customer refunds under GDPR or EU AI Act scrutiny.
What does the Deterministic Alternative score flag?
It asks whether a plain script, SQL query, BI template or rule engine can solve 80% of the job without a language model. Scoring 4 or 5 triggers a Don't Use an LLM warning because paying ongoing inference, prompt maintenance and evaluation costs for a deterministic workflow wastes budget and introduces hallucination risk where none existed.
How is the realistic annual net value calculated?
The model starts with gross time saved across 46 working weeks per year, then applies two haircuts most vendor decks skip: your per-initiative human review and QA overhead percentage, and the global Year-1 adoption rate slider. It subtracts annual licence, API and evaluation maintenance costs to show steady-state net value, and divides initial build cost by monthly net benefit to find payback months.
How does the Build vs Buy vs Configure recommendation work?
Initiatives solvable with deterministic logic get routed to plain code or SQL first. Low-value or high-risk traps get marked Defer or Kill. High-readiness, low-risk requests with modest integration costs recommend buying off-the-shelf SaaS; moderate-complexity requests recommend configuring an existing enterprise AI platform; high-governance or messy-data bets recommend a phased custom build with audit trails and human approval gates.
Does my AI portfolio data leave my browser?
No. Everything runs locally in vanilla JavaScript inside your browser and auto-saves to your device's localStorage. Nothing is posted to a backend server. If you click Copy Share URL, your portfolio is encoded inside the URL hash fragment so you can share it directly with a colleague.
What is included in the Steering Committee Pack export?
The Markdown export generates an executive-ready brief with your portfolio assumptions, headline ROI metrics, total budget saved from killed pilots, a ranked decision table, Ship Now priorities with Build vs Buy verdicts, and an explicit Kill List rationale you can paste straight into Notion, Confluence or a board paper.
How It Works
  1. Start with the 6 pre-loaded enterprise requests or add your own. Click any bubble on the 2×2 matrix or any row in the decision table to inspect its operational scores, cost assumptions and verdict.
  2. Score the five operational axes (1-5). Rate Data Readiness, Error Blast Radius, Regulatory Exposure, Adoption Friction and Deterministic Alternative to calculate a transparent 0-100 Feasibility & Safety score.
  3. Stress-test the economics. Enter affected staff, weekly runs, minutes saved, hourly rate, human QA overhead and Year-1 build plus run costs. Drag the global Year-1 adoption slider to see which pilots survive realistic rollout rates.
  4. Spot plain-code traps and governance dead ends. Any initiative scoring 4 or 5 on Deterministic Alternative gets a dashed ring and a Don't Use an LLM warning so you stop paying token bills for SQL jobs.
  5. Export your Steering Committee Pack. Copy or download the Markdown executive brief, export CSV for Finance, print a clean PDF, or share a live URL with your team.
Key Points
  • Five operational axes replace guesswork. Data Readiness (28%), Error Blast Radius (28%), Regulatory Exposure (24%) and Adoption Friction (20%) drive feasibility, while Deterministic Alternative catches non-AI problems.
  • CFO-ready haircuts by default. Calculations use 46 working weeks per year, subtract human review and exception-handling overhead, and apply your global Year-1 adoption rate before subtracting API and evaluation costs.
  • Saved From Wasted Pilot Spend metric. Quantifies the exact Year-1 build and run budget protected by killing Governance Traps and plain-code LLM requests before kick-off.
  • Built-in Build vs Buy vs Configure guidance. Prevents engineering teams from building bespoke wrappers for commodity workflows that an existing SaaS tool already covers.
  • Shareable URL state with zero backend. Portfolio changes auto-save in localStorage and can be encoded into a self-contained hash URL without sending confidential roadmap data over the wire.
  • Advisory modelling tool. Use this matrix to structure steering committee trade-offs; final regulatory classification under the EU AI Act or UK GDPR still requires qualified legal review.
